How A $25,000 Beach Cottage Became One Of The Most Iconic Homes In American History

By Kaleena Fraga | Edited By Cara Johnson
Published September 28, 2026
Updated September 29, 2026

Purchased by Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. in 1928, the Kennedy Compound on Cape Cod has been home to generations of Kennedys and even served as JFK's "Summer White House" during his presidency.

The Kennedy Compound is almost as famous as the Kennedy family itself. Located in Hyannis Port, Massachusetts, the property — which is made up of three houses set along the beaches of Cape Cod — has been the site of many important moments in the history of the political dynasty.

It was there, in 1953, that John F. Kennedy brought Jackie after proposing marriage. The Kennedy Compound also acted as a "Summer White House" in the 1960s, where President Kennedy could take strolls along the beach as he wrestled with "tough decisions." And in 1963, the president's mother, Rose, was at the home when she heard of her son's assassination.

Above, look through 33 photos of the Kennedy Compound. And below, discover how it became one of the most iconic homes in American history.

The Origin Story Of The Kennedy Compound

According to the John F. Kennedy Hyannis Port Museum, the origins of the Kennedy Compound on Cape Cod can be traced to 1926. That summer, Kennedy family patriarch Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. rented Malcolm Cottage in Hyannis Port for his young family.

Kennedy Sr. had previously tried to find a summer home in Cohasset, a coastal town about an hour from Hyannis Port. However, he was turned away from the exclusive Cohasset Golf Club, allegedly because he was Irish Catholic. When Kennedy Sr. learned that Hyannisport Club was more welcoming to Irish Catholics, he decided to give it a try.

He liked the area so much that he purchased Malcolm Cottage two years later for $25,000 (roughly $485,000 today). It would soon become known as the Kennedy Compound.

The house at Hyannis Port became a summer destination for the Kennedy family, which included Joseph, his wife Rose, and their children: Joseph Jr., John, Rosemary, Kathleen, Eunice, Patricia, Robert, Jean, and Edward.

Kennedy Family CollectionThe Kennedy family in Hyannis Port. July 1934.

The Kennedys cherished their summers on Cape Cod. As an adult, John F. Kennedy bought his own home in Hyannis Port, just behind his father's house. So did his younger brother, Robert. These three oceanside properties, which total six acres, make up what's known today as the Kennedy Compound.

The compound would be the site of great triumph — and great tragedy — for the Kennedy family over the years.

Tragedies And Triumphs In Hyannis Port

John F. Kennedy spent many happy boyhood days in Hyannis Port. And as he geared up to run for president in 1960, the Kennedy Compound became the headquarters for his presidential campaign. Indeed, JFK watched the election results at the Kennedy Compound after voting in Boston. And it was in Hyannis Port that he celebrated his victory over Richard Nixon.

Kennedy Family CollectionJohn F. Kennedy, alongside Jackie and Caroline Kennedy, on Nov. 8, 1960 — Election Day.

"Ladies and gentlemen, I have received the following wire from Vice President Nixon," John F. Kennedy, the president-elect, announced the next night at the Hyannis Armory. "In that wire he says, 'Senator John F. Kennedy, Hyannis Port, Massachusetts. I want to repeat through this wire the congratulations and best wishes I extended to you on television last night. I know that you will have united support of all Americans as you lead the nation in the cause of peace and freedom during the next four years.'"

Though his presidency took JFK to Washington, D.C., he and his family still spent their summers at the Kennedy Compound in Hyannis Port — which became known as the "Summer White House."

"Hyannis Port became, during the summers, a major national and international dateline," the John F. Kennedy Hyannis Port Museum explains. "The massive and intensive press coverage — newspapers, TV and glossy magazine color photos — of the wealthy, powerful and glamorous family at work and play in seaside settings focused national attention on Cape Cod."

Cecil Stoughton. White House Photographs. John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum, BostonPresident John F. Kennedy on the presidential yacht, the Honey Fitz, while in Hyannis Port. September 1963.

But on Nov. 22, 1963, the glamour of the Kennedy White House came to a sudden, violent end. Rose Kennedy was at the Kennedy Compound when her son Robert, then the attorney general, called her to let her know that John F. Kennedy had been shot in Dallas. Robert then called again to let her know that the president was dead.

The assassination of John F. Kennedy marked the end of an era. But the Kennedy Compound remained an important meeting place for his family.

The Kennedy Compound In Recent Years

The Kennedy family continued to spend time in Hyannis Port in the second half of the 20th century. In 1994, 30 years after the president's assassination, his son, John F. Kennedy Jr., brought his then-girlfriend, Carolyn Bessette, to the Kennedy Compound to meet his famous family.

According to The Kennedy Heirs: John, Caroline, and the New Generation — A Legacy of Tragedy and Triumph by J. Randy Taraborrelli, the meeting did not go well. JFK Jr.'s aunt Ethel (the wife of Robert Kennedy, who had been assassinated in 1968) quizzed guests on current events. Ethel asked Carolyn about the federal assault weapons ban, and when Carolyn expressed ignorance, Ethel remarked: "You may want to read up on it."

Though Carolyn purportedly swore she would never return to the Kennedy Compound, she did. In fact, she and JFK Jr. — then her husband — were flying to Hyannis Port to attend a family wedding when their plane crashed off the coast of Martha's Vineyard on July 16, 1999. The crash killed the famous couple, as well as Carolyn's sister, Lauren.

MediaPunch Inc / Alamy Stock PhotoJohn F. Kennedy Jr. and his wife, Carolyn Bessette-Kennedy, died in a plane crash while JFK Jr. was flying them to Hyannis Port for a family wedding.

Indeed, Hyannis Port was often the site of sad events. Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. died there in 1969; so did his wife, Rose, in 1995, and their son, Ted Kennedy, in 2009. But the family has also celebrated many happy occasions at the Kennedy Compound, from the engagement of JFK and Jackie back in 1953 to the marriage of Robert and Ethel's granddaughter, Sarah Kennedy, in 2023.

Now, roughly a century since Joseph P. Kennedy Sr. first bought the Main House at the Kennedy Compound, the Kennedy family continues to spend time on Cape Cod. In recent years, the entire family has gathered to celebrate the Fourth of July together in Hyannis Port.

In that way, the Kennedy Compound is still an important place. Not only is it special to the Kennedy family to this day, but the sweeping grounds of the compound, with its views of Nantucket Sound, are also imbued with history.

It was there, in 1960, that JFK waited to hear the results of the election — and celebrated with his family. It was there that he spent summers with his wife and children, taking a break from the pressures of the White House. And it was there, in 1963, that his mother walked along the beach after she heard about his assassination.

As Jackie Kennedy famously told a Life reporter from the Kennedy Compound just four days after her husband's funeral, "Don't let it be forgot, that once there was a spot, for one brief shining moment that was known as Camelot — and it will never be that way again."
