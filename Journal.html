Framing the View: Meet the 2026 Best of LaCantina Winners

The 9th Annual Best of LaCantina winners: contemporary homes where sleek, considered architecture hinges on the clarity of glazed openings.

Architizer Editors
  • Sponsored

Sponsored by

Instaread

We are thrilled to announce the winners of the 9th Annual Best of LaCantina Design Competition!

This year’s selected projects demonstrate how thoughtfully designed doors and windows shape architectural experiences, framing views, bringing natural light indoors, and creating stronger connections between buildings and their surroundings. From expansive glass openings to carefully considered transitions between interior and exterior spaces, the winning designs showcase the architectural possibilities of LaCantina‘s door systems.

Topping the 2026 list is The Still by KA DesignWorks, winner of the prestigious Best in Show award. Located in Mountain Village, Colorado, this 6,100-square-foot mountain retreat uses LaCantina Contemporary Clad doors to open its living spaces toward panoramic views of the San Sophia Range. As part of their prize, KA DesignWorks will receive an all-expenses-paid trip for two to the 2027 AIA Conference in Philadelphia and an invitation to participate in a live Architizer panel discussing the project.

Scroll on to discover all the winners of the 9th Annual Best of LaCantina competition and explore how each project approaches the relationship between architecture, its occupants, and the surrounding environment.

Best in Show + Best Rural Residential Project: The Still by KA DesignWorks, Mountain Village, Colorado

Photo by Dallas & Harris Photography

Photo by Dallas & Harris Photography

The mountains take center stage at The Still, a 6,100-square-foot residence designed by KA DesignWorks as a retreat from the pace of everyday life. Facing the San Sophia Range, the home draws on its dramatic hillside setting, using stone walls and restrained architectural forms to anchor the building to the terrain. Inside, a vaulted great room opens toward expansive northern views, while wood, stone, and glass give the interiors a warm, understated character. LaCantina’s Contemporary Clad sliding doors were selected for their slim, minimal profiles, allowing for expansive glass openings that reduce visual separation between the interior and the mountains. The large-format systems connect the living spaces to outdoor terraces while meeting the performance demands of a mountain climate, keeping the architecture open to its surroundings without compromising its clean lines.

Best Urban Residential Project: Sackett Residence by Harper Design+Build, Brooklyn, New York

Photos by Elizabeth Hope, Dooley Images

Photos by Elizabeth Hope, Dooley Images

A historic carriage house in Brooklyn gets a contemporary addition in this 4,500-square-foot renovation by Harper Design+Build. The project preserves the character of the original façade while introducing a three-story extension and a new courtyard that brings natural light deep into the home. The main living spaces are organized around the garden, with a restrained palette of natural stone and warm wood giving the interiors a calm, contemporary character. A key part of this arrangement is LaCantina’s Bronze Anodized Aluminum TC Sliding Door System, which creates a generous expanse of glass between the living area and courtyard. Its minimal profiles keep the garden in clear view, while a concealed sill integrated alongside the interior wood flooring creates a flush threshold. When open, the doors allow the courtyard to become a natural extension of the living space.

Best Suburban Residential Project: Stealth Oasis by Ogawa Fisher Architects, San Mateo, California

Photo by Vivian Johnson

Photo by Vivian Johnson

From the street, Stealth Oasis by Ogawa Fisher Architects gives little away. Set slightly below street level, the 5,100-square-foot home maintains a modest presence among its neighboring ranch houses, concealing a series of carefully considered interior spaces and a private garden beyond. Inside, the layout follows the site’s natural contours, with the kitchen stepping down toward views of the trees and bay. Nearly 12-foot-tall LaCantina folding doors open the living area directly onto the garden, with large glass panels and minimal frames keeping the views unobstructed. Selected for their ability to accommodate the doors’ exceptional height and scale, the system allows the panels to disappear into the walls when open. A covered deck clad in thermally treated ash extends the living space outdoors, creating a sheltered setting for year-round use.

Best Compact Project: La Roda Residence by Ethan Coverstone, Los Angeles, California

Photo by Stephen Archer Studio

Photo by Stephen Archer Studio

Set into the hills of Eagle Rock, La Roda Residence by Ethan Coverstone makes the most of a modest 1,179-square-foot footprint through a carefully planned renovation. With the home’s low position limiting daylight, operable glazing was introduced along the front façade to bring more natural light into the interiors. Fluted glass provides privacy from the neighboring retaining walls while allowing daylight into the kitchen. Inside, white oak floors and Douglas fir ceilings provide a warm backdrop for custom cabinetry and quartzite counters. LaCantina’s pivot front door was customized to align with the adjacent windows, while an exterior glass pocket sliding door connects the home office to the outdoors. The latter was particularly important given the limited space for conventional door swings, providing access to fresh air and natural light without sacrificing valuable floor area.

Best Renovation Project: The Cabana Concept by CABANA CONCEPTS, Oceanside, California

Photo by Austin Nield

Photo by Austin Nield

Photo by Austin Nield

The vision behind The Cabana Concept is simple: transform an everyday home into a private backyard resort—a place designed for barbecuing and entertaining, relaxing in the California sunshine, and creating lasting memories with family and friends.

The 1,850-square-foot home is organized around generous openings that connect the kitchen, dining and living areas to the pool and garden. At the center of the project, two LaCantina Aluminum Multi-Slide systems meet at a corner, with fully operable panels that allow the kitchen walls to open toward the backyard. Elsewhere, V2 Folding Doors and Aluminum Folding Windows extend this connection into the dining room, living room, and primary bedroom. Integrated Centor screens and blackout shades provide insect protection and light control. A consistent panel profile across the different LaCantina systems allowed the design team to align the openings while retaining flexibility in how each space connects to the outdoors.

Most Innovative Project: Sarah by Rossington Architecture, Mill Valley, California

Photo by Eric Rorer and Phil Rossington

Photo by Eric Rorer and Phil Rossington

Originally designed to capture views of the Mill Valley Golf Course, this 2,485-square-foot home had never fully embraced its hillside setting. A renovation by Rossington Architecture opened the interiors to the landscape through vaulted ceilings, new dormers, and larger glazed openings. One of the most significant interventions was at the kitchen, where a LaCantina Contemporary Clad corner folding door system removes the corner of the façade and directs movement toward an outdoor dining terrace beneath a new pergola. The zero-post configuration allows both sides to open without a fixed corner support, creating a direct connection between the kitchen and garden. Additional Contemporary Clad sliding and swing doors carry the same approach throughout the house. The architect selected LaCantina for its proven durability, precise detailing, and reliable weather performance, particularly important for the technical demands of a corner opening.

Best Indoor/Outdoor Concept: Montecito Midcentury by Kimberly AuBuchon Designs

Photo by Jason Rick Photography

Photo by Jason Rick Photography

A complete renovation and addition transformed this 1950s ranch into a 4,000-square-foot family home. While the original bedroom wing was carefully restored, the kitchen, living areas, and master suite were newly constructed. The former narrow galley kitchen gave way to a vaulted, open-plan space oriented toward a new backyard patio and pool. LaCantina Contemporary Clad folding doors and windows, finished in Shadow Black, play a central role in this transformation, allowing the living spaces to open fully onto the outdoors. A folding window also serves as a kitchen pass-through, with an outdoor counter providing seating for guests. Custom configurations, recessed floor tracks, and a recessed counter track accommodate the project’s specific needs, while the fold-flat panels can be tucked away when open, keeping the openings clear.

Special Mention: Carrita Seaview by JRP Design & Remodel, Malibu, California

Photo by JRP Design and Remodel

Photo by JRP Design and Remodel

Rebuilt following the Woolsey Fire, this 3,672-square-foot Malibu residence was designed to capture sweeping ocean views and reflect the homeowners’ connection to the California coast. Drawing on the character of ranch living, the home pairs a warm, natural material palette with contemporary architecture. LaCantina Aluminum Wood folding doors create expansive openings between the great room and outdoor entertaining areas, while a matching folding window connects the kitchen to the patio. Both systems feature slim sightlines that preserve views of the coastline and bring natural light deep into the interiors. Finished with bronze hardware and equipped with non-pleated screens, the systems support open-air living while addressing the demands of Malibu’s coastal climate. Together, the openings make the surrounding landscape an integral part of the home’s everyday experience.

From hillside homes to backyard retreats, this year’s winning projects show how carefully considered openings can change the way architecture engages with its surroundings.
